# What we never compromise

One idea behind all our projects: **make essential things simpler, more accessible, less wasteful, and affordable or
free wherever practical.** Each project keeps its own code, data and rules so it can move at its own pace.

These rules shape every screen and every server action. When a feature conflicts with one, the feature changes.

1. **Simple first.** One clear action per screen. RSVP is one tap and always optional; guests, notes and meal
   details stay optional extras.
2. **Everyone is welcome.** Anyone can see events, menus and how to find us without an account. Signing in is an
   email link or code — no passwords.
3. **Food safety is never implied.** Nothing is called "safe", "allergen-free" or "guaranteed". Contains, May contain
   and Related stay distinct. Votes are advice; staff confirm how food is prepared before a menu is published.
4. **Privacy first.** Dietary information is seen only by the person and, through an audited report, by kitchen staff
   and admins. Organizers see counts, not names. Anyone can delete their dietary data or whole account at any time.
5. **Strong permissions.** Every permission is checked on the server and in the database. Roles are granted by
   admins, never self-selected, and changes are audited.
6. **Honest information.** Contact details, menus and numbers show only what is confirmed. Nothing is estimated or
   invented, and sample content is labeled [DEMO].
7. **No dead ends.** Every button does something real or isn't shown. Every list has an empty state, every action
   gives feedback, and every error says what to do next.
8. **Mobile-first and accessible.** Built for a phone on the way to Shabbat dinner: fast, readable, keyboard and
   screen-reader friendly.
9. **Less waste.** One database, one host, one email sender — no services we don't need. We collect only what the
   app uses and clean up what it no longer needs.
10. **Free means free.** Using the site costs nothing, and no payment form appears anywhere. Running it stays on free
    or low-cost plans.
11. **Organizers decide.** The app supports the people who run Chabad at Lehigh. Menus, approvals and capacity are
    their calls, not the software's.
12. **Configurable, not hard-coded.** Bethlehem is where we start, not the limit. Names, the campus and the time zone
    live in `src/config/site.ts`, and contacts, addresses and student email domains in Dashboard → Settings, so another
    campus or community could adapt the app. Nothing more is built for that until real use calls for it.
